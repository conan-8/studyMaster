# Central Ideas and Details

**Slug:** `central-ideas-details` · **Domain:** Information and Ideas

## What it tests

Whether the student can identify what a short passage is mainly about — or
locate a specific stated fact within it. This is the most foundational RW
skill: comprehension of explicit and near-explicit content. On the digital
SAT, each item is a single short passage (typically 1–3 sentences, well under
150 words) with one question attached.

## Worked shape

The passage makes one dominant point and supports it with one or two
subordinate details. For main-idea items the stem is *"Which choice best
states the main idea of the text?"*; for detail items it is *"According to
the text, ..."* aimed at a specific checkable fact. The four choices are
complete statements. The correct one matches the passage's claim in scope,
subject, and direction. Wrong answers fail in nameable ways: a true detail
promoted to thesis, an overgeneralization, a reversal, or a claim the
passage never makes.

## Original example item (medium difficulty)

> Glass sponges, which anchor themselves to the deep-sea floor, build their
> skeletons from silica extracted from seawater. Because this process
> proceeds slowly and depends on a steady supply of dissolved silica, marine
> biologist Ingrid Halvorsen has proposed that the distribution of glass
> sponge reefs is limited less by water temperature than by the availability
> of silica in local currents.

**Which choice best states the main idea of the text?**

- A) Glass sponges grow more slowly in deep water than in shallow water.
- B) Halvorsen proposes that silica supply, not temperature, is the main
  constraint on where glass sponge reefs occur. ✔
- C) Silica extracted from seawater is the primary building material for
  most deep-sea organisms.
- D) Ocean currents transport the silica that most marine organisms need to
  survive.

**Why the distractors fail:** A invents a depth/growth claim never made.
C overgeneralizes from glass sponges to "most deep-sea organisms." D widens
the passage's claim about one organism group into a claim about "most marine
organisms" and drops the reef-distribution point entirely.

## Difficulty commentary

- **Easy:** explicit topic sentence up front; distractors are off-topic or
  contradicted. Solve by matching the first sentence.
- **Medium:** the main idea is spread across sentences; at least one
  distractor is a real detail from the passage wearing a thesis costume.
- **Hard:** the main idea is implied; the dangerous distractors are
  near-paraphrases of the key with one altered qualifier or scope word.
  Reading load (embedded clauses, technical terms) does much of the work.

## Generation pitfalls

1. **Two defensible answers.** If a supporting detail is phrased broadly
   enough to pass as the main idea, the item is broken. Keep details visibly
   subordinate.
2. **Outside-knowledge keys.** The correct answer must be provable from the
   passage alone — never reward what the student happens to know.
3. **Echo keys.** If the correct answer copies the passage's exact sentence,
   the item tests matching, not comprehension. Paraphrase at medium distance.
4. **Detail items with fuzzy targets.** The asked fact must be explicitly
   present; "according to the text" cannot point at an implication.
